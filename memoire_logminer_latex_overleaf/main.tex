\chapter*{Resume}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Resume}
Ce memoire presente \logminer, un prototype local multi-agents de detection d'anomalies dans des journaux systemes et reseaux heterogenes. Le systeme organise la collecte, le parsing, la normalisation, le routage multi-modeles, la detection, la correlation, l'audit, la memoire adaptative et la visualisation. Les termes autonome, distribue et agents intelligents multi-taches sont employes dans un sens operationnel : selection de taches par politique explicite, repartition locale multi-processus via Redis Streams, memoire locale et persistante, heartbeat et handlers multiples. L'objectif n'est pas de proposer un detecteur universel ni un SOC industriel, mais de valider une chaine complete transformant des logs heterogenes en evenements normalises, anomalies candidates et incidents exploitables.

L'evaluation combine journaux Windows, exports Wazuh, logs Linux/auth, HDFS, BGL, CICIDS2017 et CIC-DDoS2019. Les protocoles exploratoires produisent des F1 de 0,916602, 0,997163 et 0,999965 sur Linux/auth, CICIDS2017 et CIC-DDoS2019. Des holdouts plus stricts donnent respectivement 0,826251, 0,157827 et 0,997577, montrant une forte sensibilite de CICIDS2017 au protocole de separation. Une comparaison controlee CICIDS2017, a features, hyperparametres, seeds et tailles apparies, oppose un split aleatoire a 0,999260 et cinq holdouts fichier/scenario associes aux graines 42--46 a 0,157827 ; la dispersion mesure surtout des differences entre scenarios tenus hors entrainement. Les sorties non supervisees sont rapportees comme anomalies candidates, notamment 3 676 signaux sur 122 563 evenements Wazuh. Sur HDFS/BGL, une validation complementaire Drain3 avec templates, fenetres temporelles et split train--test montre une progression moderee sur HDFS et une separabilite tres favorable sur BGL, sans preuve de generalisation universelle. Les benchmarks locaux rapportent 8,2012 secondes de workflow moyen sur 10 micro-lots de 8 537 lignes et 9,3300 secondes sur la campagne CPU/RAM de 30 cycles. La campagne Redis de six heures valide 8 508 taches uniques, aucune tache pending finale et 709 reprises apres panne simulee. Une comparaison controlee monolithique-versus-agents ne montre pas de gain de debit sur petit volume, mais mesure l'overhead d'orchestration et la reprise apres panne.

Les resultats montrent la faisabilite d'une approche locale, legere et interpretable pour l'analyse de journaux heterogenes. La memoire adaptative constitue une capitalisation auditable des historiques et decisions analyste ; l'apprentissage actif complet reste une perspective. Les limites concernent notamment la validation multi-machine, la reduction mesuree des faux positifs, l'apprentissage continu controle et l'industrialisation en environnement SOC.

\chapter*{Abstract}
\addcontentsline{toc}{chapter}{Abstract}
This thesis presents \logminer, a modular prototype for anomaly detection in heterogeneous system and network logs. The system combines collection, parsing, normalization, family-aware routing, detection, correlation, audit, adaptive memory and visualization. Autonomy, distribution and intelligent multi-task agents are used in an operational sense: explicit task-selection policy, local multi-process distribution through Redis Streams, local and persistent memory, heartbeat and multiple handlers. The objective is not to propose a universal detector or an industrial SOC, but to validate a local end-to-end chain that transforms heterogeneous logs into normalized events, anomaly candidates and analyst-readable incidents.

The evaluation combines local Windows logs, Wazuh exports, Linux/auth logs, HDFS, BGL, CICIDS2017 and CIC-DDoS2019. Exploratory protocols produce F1-scores of 0.916602, 0.997163 and 0.999965 on Linux/auth, CICIDS2017 and CIC-DDoS2019. Stricter holdouts respectively give 0.826251, 0.157827 and 0.997577, showing strong sensitivity of CICIDS2017 to the split protocol. A controlled CICIDS2017 comparison, with matched features, hyperparameters, seeds and train/test sizes, contrasts a random split at 0.999260 with five file/scenario holdouts associated with seeds 42--46 at 0.157827; the dispersion mainly reflects differences between held-out scenarios. Non-supervised outputs are reported as anomaly candidates, including 3,676 signals over 122,563 Wazuh events. A Drain3-based train-test validation with templates and temporal windows shows moderate progress on HDFS and very favorable separability on BGL, without proving universal generalization. Local benchmarks report a mean workflow duration of 8.2012 seconds over 10 micro-batches of 8,537 lines and 9.3300 seconds over the 30-cycle CPU/RAM campaign. The six-hour Redis campaign validates 8,508 unique tasks, no final pending task and 709 recoveries after simulated failures. A controlled monolithic-versus-agents comparison does not show a throughput gain on small volume, but measures orchestration overhead and failure recovery.

The results support the feasibility of a local, lightweight and interpretable approach to heterogeneous log analysis. Adaptive memory is treated as an auditable capitalization of histories and analyst decisions, while full active learning remains future work. Remaining limitations include strict multi-machine validation, measured false-positive reduction, controlled continuous learning and industrial-scale SOC deployment.
